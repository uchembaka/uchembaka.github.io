Lectures 01 and 02 · What is statistics? Types of data

From a survey to a question

Statistics turns variable data into conclusions about a population. NHANES (the US National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey) measures people at mobile examination centres and asks them about their health. Start by naming the parts of a study and the type of each variable, since the type decides which summaries and methods are valid.

1 · Collect

NHANES is an observational study: researchers record what they find and assign no treatment.

Lectures 05 to 07 · Probability rules, random variables, the Binomial distribution

Probability and the Binomial model

Pick a characteristic. Its share of the NHANES population is the probability p that a randomly chosen adult has it. Choose n adults independently and the count Y with the characteristic follows Binomial(n, p). Simulating real groups shows the frequentist definition at work: relative frequencies settle on the model.

Two events at once: independence and the addition rule

If A and B are independent, Pr(A ∩ B) = Pr(A) × Pr(B). If they are mutually exclusive, Pr(A ∩ B) = 0. Real health variables are rarely either.

Lectures 09 and 10 · Sampling distributions, Normal approximation, Central Limit Theorem

Many samples, one distribution

Draw repeated random samples of n adults and record each sample mean (or proportion). The pile of results is the sampling distribution. It centres on the population value, its spread is the standard error σ/√n, and for n ≥ 30 it is close to Normal even when the variable itself is skewed.

Lectures 11 and 12 · Confidence intervals (z and t), margin of error, sample size

Confidence intervals that you can audit

One sample gives one interval. Over many samples, about 95% of 95% intervals contain μ. Here μ is known, so every interval that misses is marked. Use z when σ is known; use t with n − 1 degrees of freedom when σ is replaced by the sample s.

Lectures 01 to 12 together

Case study: blood pressure in US adults

A full analysis from one sample, the way you would do it with real data where the population is unknown. At the end we reveal the population value and check the conclusion.

Data: NHANES, the 2009 to 2010 and 2011 to 2012 cycles, as packaged in the R package NHANES (Pruim, CRAN), restricted to adults aged 20 and over (7,235 rows). The package resamples respondents so that the data approximate the US population, which is why some rows repeat. Tables quoted (Φ, z, t) follow the New Cambridge Statistical Tables used in the lectures, and z multipliers for confidence intervals use the lecture values 1.65, 1.96 and 2.58.

Quartiles use the lecture rule (median of each half), which matches R's fivenum().
